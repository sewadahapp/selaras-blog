Naya Arunika is an independent musician and producer based in Bandung, Indonesia. Her work moves between ambient electronics, intimate pop, field recordings, and experimental sound — usually all four at once, usually after dark.

## How the records are made

The records begin small. A semi-modular synthesizer and a sampler live in a spare room in the Dago hills, where two microphones stay pointed at whatever the room is doing. An effects rack older than the project patches everything through a chain that hums a little out of tune. Naya keeps the hum.

![An effects rack with a Yamaha SPX90 and a Peavey DPM Spectrum in a home studio. Photograph by Klara Kopf, CC BY 2.0.](/images/effects-rack-yamaha-spx90-peavey-dpm-spectrum.jpg)

> "A room tone after the lights go out is the most honest recording you will ever get."

She writes, produces, and performs her own music, and collaborates with filmmakers, dancers, and visual artists on sound-led projects. The rule is the same in both cases: start from what the space already sounds like, then try not to ruin it.

## The small-sound habit

The habit is unglamorous. A recorder rides along on the first commuter train; the rain against the studio window gets its own folder; a market's closing hour is kept the way other producers keep presets. What the city gives away for free ends up carrying more of each record than anything that gets bought.

![Bandung's station platforms late in the evening, lights on and mostly empty. Photograph by Midori, CC BY 3.0.](/images/bandung-station-platform.jpg)

## What you'll read here

The journal is a notebook in public: work-in-progress mixed alongside the finished things, because the middle of a process is usually the interesting part. Expect longer notes on how records come together, short observations from the city at night, and the occasional track diary — everything worth keeping from a slow way of making things.
